# Development Update - 2026-08-10

## Focus Of The Day

Added a fourth computer difficulty, **Expert**, backed by a real depth search —
paranoid alpha-beta with iterative deepening — alongside the existing one-ply
greedy opponent. The hard requirement going in was that the game must not become
slower or janky, and that requirement shaped every decision below.

## What Landed

### 1. `lib/engine/search.ts`

Paranoid alpha-beta with iterative deepening, move ordering, and a **node budget**
rather than a time budget.

- **Paranoid, not maxn.** Minimax is a two-player theorem and this game seats
  eight. Every seat that is not the mover is collapsed into one opponent
  minimising the mover's score. Maxn is more truthful but prunes almost nothing,
  because alpha-beta needs a single scalar to bound against.
- **Nodes, not milliseconds.** The engine may not read the clock — it is pure so
  the same code can run in the browser, in Node tests, and in an authoritative
  server. A node budget is deterministic, which also means a seeded test is
  reproducible and a server and a client cannot disagree about the best move
  because one of them happened to be busy.
- **Iterative deepening turns the budget into a guarantee.** The search completes
  depth 1, then 2, then 3, keeping the best move it has; when the budget runs out
  it plays the deepest completed answer. A small board gets more plies than a
  large one for the same cost, automatically.

### 2. A flat simulation, because the engine was too slow to search

`applyMove` sustains ~88k positions/sec on 6x6 and ~54k on 14x14, because it
clones every cell as an object, copies the player array, and allocates a fresh
neighbour list per cell per cascade pass. At that rate a depth-3 search on the
XXL board takes most of a second — a frozen tab.

The search therefore runs on a flat mirror: typed arrays for counts and owners, a
precomputed neighbour table, one pre-allocated frame per ply, and an incremental
per-player orb tally so the single-owner cascade guard is O(players) rather than
O(cells). That measures **0.5M–1.1M positions/sec**, six to twelve times faster,
which is the difference between depth 2 and depth 5.

### 3. The duplicate-rules hazard, handled mechanically

Two implementations of the rules can drift apart silently. That is the one real
risk this change takes on, so it is covered by a test rather than by care:
`__tests__/search.test.ts` replays random games through both implementations and
compares every cell, every elimination flag and the winner after **every legal
move**, not just the one played — across 6x6, 8x8, a rectangular 5x9, and 2, 3
and 4 players.

It found nothing on the first run, which is the outcome worth having; the value
is that a future rule change cannot land on only one side.

## Measurements

Budget sweep, 40 distinct mid-game positions per board, JIT warm:

| Budget | 6x6 worst | 10x10 worst | 14x14 worst | Depth |
|--------|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| 6k     | 10ms      | 14ms        | 19ms        | 3–5   |
| 12k    | 20ms      | 27ms        | 24ms        | 3–5   |
| 24k    | 39ms      | 59ms        | 54ms        | 3–5   |

`DEFAULT_MAX_NODES` is **12,000**. Doubling it buys one extra ply on the 10x10
board alone and costs twice the latency everywhere, which is the wrong trade on
the mid-range Android the product brief calls out.

**Strength.** Expert beat Hard **120 games to 0** — 60 on Classic 6x6, 60 on HD
10x10, alternating the opening seat so first-move advantage could not flatter
either side. Expert beat Normal 40–0. The committed regression asserts a
conservative 80% so a tie-break cannot fail CI while a real regression still does.

**Latency in a real browser.** Eight Expert bots on the 14x14 board, profiled with
a `longtask` PerformanceObserver over 30 seconds: **zero long tasks**. The Hard
control produced one, 69ms, at t=0.0s — page startup, not the opponent.

## Decisions Worth Recording

- **Expert was added beside Hard, never on top of it.** A player who settled on
  Hard should keep getting the opponent they know; a difficulty that silently
  gets stronger is a bad surprise rather than a feature. If a fifth level lands,
  it extends the ladder the same way.
- **Evaluation was not touched.** Weights moved to `lib/engine/weights.ts` so the
  heuristic and the search share one source of truth, but the terms and their
  values are unchanged. Depth and evaluation are separable; changing both at once
  would make a strength change unattributable to either.
- **The search stays on the main thread** only because it is bounded. If a future
  level needs more depth than the budget allows, the answer is a Web Worker, not
  a bigger ceiling.
